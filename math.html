Why Pharmacology Math Matters

Medical assistants must accurately calculate medication amounts to ensure patients receive the correct dose.

Always double-check your calculations before administering medication.

💊 Dosage Formula

D ÷ H × Q = X

D = Desired Dose
The amount ordered by the provider.

H = Have
The strength of medication available.

Q = Quantity
The form the medication comes in.

X = Amount to Give
The final amount administered.

Example: Tablet Calculation

Order: 500 mg

Available: 250 mg tablets

Formula:

500 ÷ 250 × 1 = 2

Answer: Give 2 tablets

Example: Liquid Medication

Order: 250 mg

Available: 125 mg per 5 mL

Formula:

250 ÷ 125 × 5 = 10 mL

Answer: Give 10 mL

Fractions in Medication Math

Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerator (top number) by the numerator. Multiply the denominator (bottom number) by the denominator. Then reduce your answer.

Example:

2/3 × 9/8

18/24 = 3/4

Dividing Fractions

Remember:

Keep → Change → Flip

  • Keep the first fraction.
  • Change division to multiplication.
  • Flip the second fraction.

Decimal Conversions

To change a fraction into a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator.

Example:

1 ÷ 4 = 0.25

To change a decimal into a percent, move the decimal two places to the right.

0.75 = 75%

⭐ Practice Problems

Question 1

A patient needs 600 mg. The medication available is 200 mg tablets. How many tablets should be given?

600 ÷ 200 × 1 = 3 tablets

Question 2

Convert 2000 mg to grams.

Answer: 2 g

Question 3

Convert 3000 mL to liters.

Answer: 3 L

⭐ Exam Tips

  • Write the formula before solving.
  • Always check your units.
  • Convert measurements carefully.
  • Do not round too early.
  • Double-check your final answer.
