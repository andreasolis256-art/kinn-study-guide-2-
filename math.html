Dosage Calculation Formula

D ÷ H × Q = X

D = Desired Dose

The amount of medication the provider ordered.

H = Have (Dose on Hand)

The amount of medication available.

Q = Quantity

The form the medication comes in (tablet, mL, etc.).

X = Amount to Give

The final answer you administer to the patient.

Example Problems

Example 1: Tablets

Order: 500 mg

Available: 250 mg tablets

500 ÷ 250 × 1 = 2

Answer: Give 2 tablets

Example 2: Liquid Medication

Order: 250 mg

Available: 125 mg per 5 mL

250 ÷ 125 × 5 = 10

Answer: Give 10 mL

Metric Conversions

Measurement Equivalent
1000 milligrams (mg) 1 gram (g)
1000 grams (g) 1 kilogram (kg)
1000 milliliters (mL) 1 liter (L)
10 millimeters (mm) 1 centimeter (cm)
100 centimeters (cm) 1 meter (m)

Household Measurements

  • 3 teaspoons (tsp) = 1 tablespoon (Tbsp)
  • 2 tablespoons = 1 ounce (oz)
  • 8 ounces = 1 cup
  • 2 cups = 1 pint
  • 2 pints = 1 quart
  • 4 quarts = 1 gallon

Fraction Review

Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the top numbers together and multiply the bottom numbers together. Then reduce your answer.

Example:

2/3 × 9/8

18/24 = 3/4

Dividing Fractions

Remember:

Keep → Change → Flip

Keep the first fraction. Change division to multiplication. Flip the second fraction.

Practice Questions

1. A medication order is 600 mg. The bottle contains 200 mg tablets. How many tablets are given?

Answer: 600 ÷ 200 × 1 = 3 tablets

2. Convert 1000 mg into grams.

Answer: 1 gram

3. Convert 2000 mL into liters.

Answer: 2 liters

⭐ Test Tips

  • Always identify what the question is asking for.
  • Write the formula before solving.
  • Check units (mg, g, mL).
  • Always reduce fractions when needed.
  • Double-check if your answer makes sense.
