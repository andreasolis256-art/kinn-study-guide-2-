What is Pharmacology?

Pharmacology is the study of drugs and how they interact with the body.

It includes learning how medications are:

  • Developed
  • Classified
  • Administered
  • Absorbed by the body
  • Used to treat diseases

What is a Drug?

A drug is a substance used to diagnose, prevent, treat, or manage a health condition.

Purposes of Medications

  • Prevent: Helps stop disease or illness.
  • Diagnose: Helps identify a medical condition.
  • Treat: Helps improve or manage a condition.
  • Cure: Removes the cause of a disease.

Drug Names

1. Chemical Name

The scientific description of the drug's chemical structure.

2. Generic Name

The official, non-brand name of a medication.

Example: Ibuprofen

3. Brand (Trade) Name

The name given by the manufacturer.

Example: Advil or Motrin

Drug Sources

  • Plant Sources: Drugs originally obtained from plants.
  • Animal Sources: Medications derived from animals.
  • Mineral Sources: Drugs containing natural minerals.
  • Synthetic Drugs: Manufactured in laboratories.
  • Biologic Drugs: Made from living organisms.

How Drugs Affect the Body

✅ Therapeutic Effect

The desired or intended effect of a medication.

Example: A pain reliever reducing pain.

⚠️ Side Effect

An expected but unwanted effect of a medication.

Example: Drowsiness from a medication.

🚨 Adverse Effect

A harmful or unexpected reaction that may require medical attention.

❗ Allergic Reaction

An immune system response to a medication.

Possible signs:

  • Rash
  • Itching
  • Swelling
  • Difficulty breathing

Medication Routes

Route Meaning
PO By mouth (oral)
SL Under the tongue
Buccal Between cheek and gum
Topical Applied to skin
Transdermal Medication absorbed through skin
IM Intramuscular injection
SubQ Subcutaneous injection
ID Intradermal injection

Controlled Substances

Controlled substances are medications regulated by the DEA because of their potential for misuse or dependence.

Schedule Description
I No accepted medical use and highest abuse potential
II High abuse potential but accepted medical use
III Moderate dependence potential
IV Lower abuse potential
V Lowest abuse potential

⭐ Test Review

  • Know the difference between generic and brand names.
  • Know therapeutic vs side effects vs adverse effects.
  • Memorize common medication routes.
  • Understand why controlled substances are regulated.
  • Know how medications affect the body.
