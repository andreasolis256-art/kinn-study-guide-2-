Flashcard 1

What is pharmacology?

The study of drugs, their effects on the body, and how they are used to treat conditions.

Flashcard 2

What is the generic name of a drug?

The official non-brand name of a medication.

Flashcard 3

What is the brand name?

The name given to a medication by the manufacturer.

Flashcard 4

What is the dosage formula?

D ÷ H × Q = X
D = Desired dose
H = Have (dose available)
Q = Quantity
X = Amount to give

Flashcard 5

What is a therapeutic effect?

The intended or desired effect of a medication.

Flashcard 6

What is a side effect?

An expected but unwanted effect of a medication.

Flashcard 7

What are the six rights of medication administration?

Right patient, right medication, right dose, right route, right time, and right documentation.

Flashcard 8

What angle is used for intramuscular injections?

90 degrees.

Flashcard 9

What angle is used for intradermal injections?

5°–15°.

Flashcard 10

What does PO mean?

By mouth (oral route).

Flashcard 11

What does PRN mean?

As needed.

Flashcard 12

What is the purpose of documentation?

To provide a legal record that medication was safely administered.

Medical Assistant Pharmacology Study Guide

Flashcard 13

What is a chemical name?

The scientific name that describes the chemical structure of a drug.

Flashcard 14

What is an adverse effect?

A harmful or unexpected reaction to a medication that may require medical attention.

Flashcard 15

What is an allergic reaction?

An immune system response to a medication. Signs may include rash, itching, swelling, or difficulty breathing.

Flashcard 16

What does IM mean?

Intramuscular — medication injected into muscle tissue.

Flashcard 17

What does SubQ mean?

Subcutaneous — medication injected into the fatty tissue under the skin.

Flashcard 18

What does ID mean?

Intradermal — medication injected into the dermis layer of the skin.

Flashcard 19

What angle is used for Subcutaneous injections?

45° or 90° depending on needle length and amount of fatty tissue.

Flashcard 20

What is pharmacokinetics?

The study of how the body affects a drug, including absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME).

Flashcard 21

What are the four processes of pharmacokinetics?

Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion (ADME).

Flashcard 22

What is absorption?

The process of a drug entering the bloodstream.

Flashcard 23

What is metabolism?

The process of the body chemically changing a drug, mainly occurring in the liver.

Flashcard 24

What is excretion?

The removal of drugs from the body, mainly through the kidneys.

Flashcard 25

What does BID mean?

Twice daily.

Flashcard 26

What does TID mean?

Three times daily.

Flashcard 27

What does QID mean?

Four times daily.

Flashcard 28

What does STAT mean?

Immediately.

Flashcard 29

What should you do if a patient refuses medication?

Respect the patient's decision, notify the provider according to policy, and document the refusal.

Flashcard 30

Why are controlled substances regulated?

Because they have potential for misuse, abuse, or dependence and are controlled by regulations.
